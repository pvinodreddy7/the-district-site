A khata, B khata and e-khata explained

Three terms every plot buyer in Karnataka hears, and what each one means for loans, registration and building.

What a khata is

A khata is the account of a property in the records of the local authority. It identifies the property and its owner for property tax. It is not the title deed, but lenders, the registration office and plan approval authorities all ask for it.

A khata and B khata

A khataThe property is on the regular register. It has its approvals in place, and the owner can get plan approvals and standard loans.
B khataA separate register for properties that are non-compliant in some way, for example lacking approvals or with deviations. Loans and plan approvals are restricted until the property is regularised.

In gram panchayat areas the records are issued through the state's e-Swathu portal, where Form 9 is the document commonly called the A khata.

E-khata

E-khata is the digital version of the khata, issued by the local authority with a unique property number. Karnataka has made e-khata a requirement for registering property, so a sale deed cannot be registered without it.

Quick answers

Is a khata the same as a title deed?

No. The khata is the property's account in the local authority's records, used for property tax. Ownership is shown by the registered sale deed.

Can a plot be registered without e-khata in Karnataka?

Karnataka has made e-khata a requirement for property registration.

What khata do plots at The District have?

Each plot comes with an electronic A khata (e-khata).
