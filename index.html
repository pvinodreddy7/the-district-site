Infrastructure

The 80,000-seat cricket stadium coming up at Suryanagar

Karnataka has started work on what is planned as India's second-largest cricket stadium, in Anekal taluk.

An international cricket stadium with a planned capacity of 80,000 is being built at Suryanagar 4th Phase in Anekal taluk, on the south-eastern edge of Bengaluru. The Chief Minister laid the foundation stone on 23 May 2026.

Once complete it is expected to be the second-largest cricket stadium in the country, after the Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad. The project is being developed and funded by the Karnataka Housing Board, without a direct allocation from the state budget.

The stadium itself has been estimated at about ₹943 crore. It sits within a wider sports complex that was announced in 2025 at about ₹1,650 crore, with facilities for other indoor and outdoor sports.

Large venues tend to pull roads, public transport, hotels and retail towards them over time. That said, a foundation stone is the start of a project, not the end of one. Timelines for projects of this size often move, so treat the stadium as a long-term factor.

What it means for The District

The District is ~1.5 km from the stadium site. For a plot buyer, that means being close to a major public investment while living on an internal road, off the main traffic.

Education

IIM Bangalore's second campus in Jigani

One of India's best-known management schools is building its undergraduate campus in the Jigani and Anekal belt.

IIM Bangalore is developing a second campus of about 110 acres at Mahanthalingapura, Jigani, roughly 27 to 30 km from its main campus on Bannerghatta Road. The first building there, a management development centre, was inaugurated in March 2023.

The campus is the home of the institute's first undergraduate programmes: four-year BSc (Honours) degrees in Economics and in Data Science. The first batch has 80 students, 40 in each programme, starting in the 2026 to 2027 academic year.

The institute has said the cohort is projected to grow to 640 students by 2031 as more of the campus is built. In August 2026 it signed an agreement with Tata Trusts to establish the undergraduate school, which supports the development of the new campus.

A residential campus of this standing brings faculty, staff, students and visiting families into the area, along with the housing, food and services they need.

What it means for The District

The District is in the same Jigani belt as the new campus. An anchor institution like this adds a long-term source of demand to an area better known for industry.

Employment

The Jigani and Bommasandra industrial corridor

The jobs that drive housing demand in this part of South Bengaluru.

Jigani and Bommasandra are two of the industrial areas of Anekal taluk, alongside Attibele, Chandapura, Electronic City and Sarjapura. They are joined by the Bommasandra and Jigani link road, a stretch of about 6 to 7 km that connects through to Electronic City.

The link road is home to large pharmaceutical and technology employers. Companies reported along it include Biocon, Cipla, Micro Labs, Mylan Laboratories and HCL Technologies. Jigani's industrial area has also drawn names such as Otis and Toyota.

The industrial land here is largely built out. A Deccan Herald report on new industrial parks noted that estates including Bommasandra, Jigani, Attibele and Electronics City, together more than 3,300 acres, have run out of space.

It is worth being candid about the other side. Residents and businesses have complained about potholes, lighting and drainage on the link road, so everyday commuting comfort depends on continued civic work.

What it means for The District

The District is a short drive from these employers. People who work on the corridor are the natural buyers and tenants for homes built on plots here.

Connectivity

The Satellite Town Ring Road and the Hosur, Anekal and Kanakapura stretch

What the ring road is, what is open today, and what is coming on the southern side.

The Satellite Town Ring Road (STRR, NH 948A) is a 280.8 km access-controlled highway being built by the National Highways Authority of India around Bengaluru. It is planned to connect 12 satellite towns, including Hoskote, Sarjapura, Attibele, Anekal, Kanakapura, Ramanagara and Magadi, and to extend to Hosur in Tamil Nadu.

Its main purpose is to let freight and inter-state traffic bypass the city. An 80 km section from Dobbaspet to Hoskote was inaugurated on 11 March 2024.

The Hoskote to Hosur stretch has been delayed by a railway overbridge. Recent reports say a 21 km section may open by December, with the overbridge work running until March 2027.

On the southern and western side, running from Hosur through Anekal and Kanakapura towards Ramanagara, the remaining packages have been tendered, including one from Thattekere in Kanakapura taluk to Bagganadoddi in Anekal taluk. This part is not open yet, and dates have slipped before, so buyers should treat its timing as uncertain.

What it means for The District

When the southern stretch is built, the Anekal belt gets a direct, signal-free route towards Hosur on one side and Kanakapura and Mysuru Road on the other, without going through the city.

Nature

Bannerghatta National Park on your doorstep

A protected forest, a zoo and safaris, within reach of the Jigani belt.

Bannerghatta National Park lies in the Anekal hills south of Bengaluru and was declared a national park in 1974. It is one of the few places where protected forest survives this close to a large city, with wildlife that includes elephants, leopards and sloth bears.

The part most families visit is Bannerghatta Biological Park, which was separated from the national park in 2002. It covers about 732 hectares and includes a zoo, safaris, a butterfly park and a rescue centre.

The park is ringed by an eco-sensitive zone, where certain industries and activities are restricted. The zone notified in March 2020 covers 168.84 sq km and is 100 m to 1 km wide. That was a reduction from an earlier draft of about 269 sq km, which conservation groups have challenged.

For residents nearby, the forest means greener surroundings and weekend outings. For buyers anywhere near the park boundary, it is also a reason to check that a layout has its approvals in place.

What it means for The District

The District is an approved layout with RERA registration, in a part of South Bengaluru where the forest and its buffer help keep the area green.

Nature

Miyawaki forests in a residential layout

What a pocket forest is, what it does well, and what to ask before you count on one.

A Miyawaki forest is a small, dense plantation of native trees and shrubs, named after the Japanese botanist Akira Miyawaki. The soil is loosened and enriched first. Saplings then go in at about three to five per square metre, far closer than in a garden, so they compete for light and grow upward.

The small footprint is why the idea suits housing. One common format fits about 600 trees into 200 sq m, roughly the size of a tennis court. In a layout, a corner of a park can become a shaded, walk-through thicket that screens the road and brings birds back, where a lawn would do neither.

The first two to three years decide the result. The saplings need watering, weeding and mulching in that period, and a plantation left alone too early does poorly. In Chennai, a spot check of civic pocket forests found some with litter and no watering a few years after planting.

The evidence is thinner than the marketing. A 2026 review in the Journal of Applied Ecology examined 51 publications on the method and found that fewer than half gave any measurements. The claim of much faster growth was partly supported; several other claims had little data behind them. Ecologists in Bengaluru add that the city's natural vegetation is dry deciduous forest and scrub, so the choice of species matters more than the density.

What it means for The District

A Miyawaki forest is being created as part of the park amenities at The District. When you visit, ask the sales team three things: which native species, who waters it for the first three years, and who maintains it after handover.
