Plot loan vs home loan

A plot loan works differently from a home loan. Here is what changes: how much you can borrow, for how long, and what you can claim.

The differences at a glance

Plot loanHome loan
How much the lender fundsUsually 70% to 80% of the plot valueUsually 75% to 90% of the property value
TenureUsually up to 15 years, sometimes 20Up to 30 years
Tax deductionsNone on the plot purchase aloneOn principal (Section 80C) and interest (Section 24)
What it can buyA residential plot in an approved layoutA ready or under-construction home, or construction

These are typical ranges published by lenders. Your own terms depend on the lender, your income and your credit record.

What that means for your budget

  • Plan for a bigger down payment: about 20% to 30% of the plot value, plus stamp duty and registration.
  • A shorter tenure means a higher EMI for the same loan amount.
  • Lenders fund plots only in layouts approved for residential use, which is one more reason to check the approvals.

Building later: the composite loan

Some lenders offer a composite loan that covers the plot and the construction of a house on it. Tax deductions on a loan for a plot generally become available once the house is built, under the same sections as a home loan.

Quick answers

How much of a plot's value will a bank lend?

Lenders usually fund 70% to 80% of the plot value. The exact figure depends on the lender and your eligibility.

Do plot loans get tax benefits?

Not on the plot purchase alone. Deductions generally become available once a house is built on the plot.

Is The District approved for plot loans?

Yes. The project is approved by all major banks for plot loans.
